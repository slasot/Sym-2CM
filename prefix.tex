\section{Introduction}

Counter machines (\cm{s}) are a particularly simple
model of computation \cite{Minsky67}.
A counter machine has a number of registers, called \emph{counters},
each register storing a natural number.
Although the instruction set may vary, 
a common one consists of: counter increment, 
counter decrement, and counter zero-test. 
Many variants of the model have been introduced and studied,
and both deterministic and nondeterministic 
machines are widely studied in the literature 
(often under different names, for instance 
\emph{counter automata}).
Numerous syntactic restrictions of the model are also widely
studied \cite{HKOW09,Ibarra78,HopcroftPansiot,FS00}
since,
as shown already by Minsky in the early 1960s \cite{Minsky61},
in its full generality the model is Turing-complete:
deterministic 2-counter machines can faithfully simulate Turing machines
and have an undecidable halting problem. 

The purpose of this paper is to understand the impact of
\emph{symmetry} on the expressive power of \cm{s}. %counter machines.
We investigate the \emph{nondeterministic} model, where
a machine may choose between different instructions executable
in the same state.

\para{Symmetric counter machines}
As the starting point, we specifically choose the set of 
machine instructions:
counter \emph{updates} that simultaneously modify all counters  
by corresponding deltas, and counter \emph{zero-tests}, which are 
executable only if a given counter has value zero.
Every \emph{transition} $(q,i,q')$ of a machine changes its state
from $q$ to $q'$,
and executes an attached instruction $i$.
Executing a transition in a given configuration constitutes a 
\emph{step} of a machine.
The model we investigate is thus essentially \vass
(vector addition systems with states) \cite{HopcroftPansiot}
extended with zero-tests, and
is clearly equivalent to standard \cm{s}. %counter machines.
In this paper we mostly focus on \cm{s} %counter machines 
that are 
\emph{symmetric}, by which we mean that the set of transitions
is invariant under all permutations $\sigma$ of counters:
for every transition $(q,i,q')$, the machine has also a transition
$(q,\sigma(i), q')$, where $\sigma(i)$ is the result of 
applying $\sigma$ to the counters in the instruction $i$.
Specifically, we choose to impose the invariance only on update transitions
(when the instruction $i$ is a counter update), but
not on zero-test transitions, as the latter restriction is not 
necessary for our proofs to work.
Here is an illustrative example of a 2-\cm (a \cm having two counters)
with
two control states and
six transitions, including five symmetric update transitions
(invariant under the swap of counters) and one zero-test transition:

\para{Contribution}
The initial motivation of this study is the immense impact of symmetry
on complexity of the \vass reachability problem:
while the problem is {\sc Ackermann}-complete in general \cite{LS19,CO21,L21},
the complexity drops to
only \pspace-complete for symmetric \vass \cite{KL25}.
As our main result, we confirm the immense drop in case of 
2-\cm{s}
(in this case, symmetry amounts to invariance under all swaps of 
counters).
We prove decidability of the configuration-to-configuration reachability
problem, where one asks if a given machine has a sequence of steps
starting in a given source configuration and ending in
a given target one:

\begin{theorem} \label{thm:main}
The reachability problem is decidable for symmetric 2-counter machines.
\end{theorem}

Symmetric two-counter machines are thus not Turing-complete.
We find this result quite surprising, as it applies to a model
with restricted update instructions but unrestricted zero-test instructions.
%

% In the proof we do not need symmetry of zero-test transitions,

Interestingly, the converse restriction, namely imposing symmetry on
zero-test transitions but not on update transitions, remains 
undecidable.

\smallskip

It is natural to consider invariance under subgroups
of permutations: for every subgroup $G\leq \symg d$
one can consider the class of \emph{$G$-counter machines}
($G$-\cm{s}), namely 
$d$-counter machines invariant under all permutations from $G$.
On one end, 
when $G=\symg d$, the general definition instantiates to 
symmetric $d$-\cm{s}.
On the opposite end, when $G$ is the trivial group one gets classical counter
machines.
Between the two extremities there is a landscape of models, 
whose expressive power is related according to the permutation subgroup relation:
whenever $G\leq H$, every $H$-\cm is automatically a
$G$-\cm.
For instance, every $\symg 3$-\cm is automatically
a $\cycg 3$-\cm, for the cyclic 
permutation group $\cycg 3 \leq \symg 3$.
As an additional result, we identify a bound on the impact of symmetry,
already for 3-counter machines, namely we prove undecidability 
for $\cycg 3$-counter machines:

\begin{theorem} \label{thm:undecid}
The reachability problem  is undecidable for $\cycg 3$-counter machines.
\end{theorem}

\para{Open questions}

A fundamental question is whether \Cref{thm:main} extends to larger numbers 
of counters. 
In particular, is the reachability problem still decidable
for symmetric 3-counter machines, whose update transitions are invariant
under all 6 permutations of the 3 counters?
Our decidability proof does not extend to 3 counters, 
as the argument relies on a
number of low-dimensional properties of reachable sets and reachability
relations.
We thus leave this intriguing question for further research, conjecturing
decidability for any number of counters:

\begin{conjecture}
The reachability problem  is decidable for symmetric counter machines.
\end{conjecture}

We focus on decidability, thus
leaving open the exact complexity of the reachability problem for 
symmetric 2-\cm{s}.
While one easily gets an elementary upper bound for our decision 
procedure (triple exponential time),
as the lower bound we only know
\pspace-hardness inherited from symmetric 2-\vass, if updates are
encoded in binary \cite{KL25}.
It is possible that the reachability problem is \pspace-complete
in symmetric $d$-\cm{s} for every $d\geq 2$.
% \mei{I'm 90\% sure that this should be $d \geq 2$.}

Symmetric \cm{s} where the only update instructions are increments 
or decrements of a single counter, constitute a syntactic fragment of our 
setting. 
It remains to be established the 
expressive power of the fragment, and the complexity of 
its reachability problem.


\para{Related research}

Identifying decidable restrictions of counter machines
is a very active line of research since 1960s, for instance 
1-counter, reversal-bounded, or restricted 2-counter machines 
\cite{HKOW09,Ibarra78,FS00}.
A prominent example is counter machines without zero-tests, that is
vector addition systems with states
(\vass), shown to have decidable reachability in early 1980s
\cite{Mayr81,Kosaraju82,Lambert92}.
The decidability extends to 
\cm{s}
with only one zero-tested counter \cite{Bonnet11},
or with nested zero-tests \cite{Reinhardt08,GCL25}.
On the other hand, most of natural restrictions of the counter model
preserve Turing-completeness, as long as no constraint is imposed
on zero-tests.
For instance, considering deterministic \cm{s}, 
the reachability problem is still undecidable
in the subclass of \emph{reversible} \cm{s},
where the step relation is reverse deterministic, and hence every step
may be uniquely undone \cite{Morita96}.

In the special case of 1 counter the symmetry requirement is void,
and the reachability problem is \np-complete 
if transition updates are encoded in binary, and \nl-complete 
in unary encoding \cite{HKOW09}.
The known complexity bounds separate symmetric 2-\cm{s} from 1-\cm{s}, 
the former being \pspace-hard while the latter \np-complete,
thus proving that symmetric 2-\cm{s} are not (easily) simulated
by 1-\cm{s}.

Restricting the use of zero-tests in 2-counter machines often
leads not only to decidability, but even 
to \pspace-completeness of the reachability problem.
Assuming binary encoding,
the \pspace-completeness has been obtained for 2-\vass 
\cite{Blondin15,BEFGHLMT21}
(assuming unary encoding, we have \nl-completeness \cite{ELT16}),
but also for the further restriction to symmetric 2-\vass \cite{KL25},
and for a relaxation of 2-\vass to 
2-counter machines with just one zero-tested counter \cite{FLS18}.
In all the three cases the reachability relation is effectively semilinear, which
is a key for obtaining the \pspace upper complexity bound, and which 
fails when the number of counters is at least 3 (even for 3-\vass) 
\cite{HopcroftPansiot}.
We do not know the exact complexity of symmetric 2-counter machines,
but it is possible that the complexity remains \pspace.
Furthermore, we do not know if the reachability relation of a
symmetric 2-counter machine is semilinear, but we believe it is so.

Contrarily to the above-mentioned restrictions of zero-tests, 
our model leaves the zero-tests unrestricted, which makes
\Cref{thm:main} particularly interesting.
We conclude by recalling another recently obtained decidability result that
also does not assume any explicit restriction of zero-tests \cite{Dudenhefner22}.
% , and hence seems as surprising as our \Cref{thm:main}.
It amounts to observing that
the above-mentioned Turing-completeness of 
reversible counter machines seems to be very fragile with respect
to the choice of instruction set of a \cm.
As shown in \cite{Dudenhefner22},
for certain 
choice of instruction set, the Turing-complete model of 2-\cm{s}
becomes decidable under the reversibility restriction.

\section{Symmetric counter machines} \label{sec:model}

Let $d$ be a positive integer.
A \emph{$d$-counter machine} ($d$-\cm in short) 
$\mach$
is a finite-state automaton whose transitions
may perform two kinds of instructions,
namely update and zero-test \emph{instructions},
on $d$ \emph{counters}, namely nonnegative integer values
stored as part of the configuration.
Formally, we define such a machine as 
$\mach = (Q,T)$, where $Q$ is a finite set of control states,
$T\subseteqfin Q\times\Instr\times Q$ is a finite set of 
\emph{transitions} of $\mach$,
and the set $\Instr$ of \emph{instructions} is the union of
update instructions and zero-test instructions:
\[
\Instr \ = \ \setof{\updinstr{w}}{w\in\Z^d} \ \cup \ 
\setof{\ztinstr{i}}{i\in \setto d}.
\]
% \mei{it is ambiguous here whether $i \in d$ is supposed to mean
% $\{0, ..., d-1\}$ or $\{1, ..., d\}$. We use the more explicit notation
% below, maybe we should do so here as well?}
Execution of $\updinstr{w}$ amounts to adding
the vector
$w$ to the counters, and execution of 
$\ztinstr{i}$ amounts to checking that counter $i$
has value 0.
Formally, we define the semantics of $\mach$ by considering
its \emph{configurations} $(q,v) \in Q\times \N^d$, written $q(v)$, and
the step relation between configurations determined by 
transitions of $\mach$:
$q(v) \step q'(v')$ if 

\begin{itemize}
\item 
$v' = v + w$,  for some  $(q,\updinstr{w},q')\in T$, or
\item  
$v' = v$ and $v(i)=0$, for some 
$(q,\ztinstr i, q')\in T$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
We use the term $d$-\vass (vector addition system with states)
for a $d$-\cm without zero-test instructions.

The symmetric group $\symg d$ acts naturally on vectors in $\Z^d$
and hence also on update transitions:
for $\sigma\in \symg d$ and $t=(q,\updinstr w,q')\in T$, we define
$\sigma(t)=(q,\updinstr{\sigma(w)},q')$, where $\sigma(w)(\sigma(i))=w(i)$
for every $i\in\setto d$.
A $d$-\cm $\mach$ is \emph{symmetric} if its set of update transitions
is invariant under the natural action: for every $\sigma\in \symg d$ 
and $(q,\updinstr w,q')\in T$, we have $(q,\updinstr {\sigma (w)},q')\in T$.
Importantly, 
we note that no restriction is imposed on zero-test transitions of $\mach$.
% We assume, \mywlog, that every zero-test applies to a single counter.
% 
In the sequel
we mostly focus on symmetric 2-\cm{s},
which are 2-\cm{s} whose set of transitions is invariant under the
swap of counters.

The reflexive-transitive closure $\step^*$ of $\step$ we call
the \emph{reachability relation} (between configurations).
The \emph{reachability problem} asks, given a \cm $\mach$ and two
of its configurations, source $s\in Q\times\N^2$ and target $t\in Q\times\N^2$, 
whether 
$s \step^* t$
(in which case we say that $t$ is \emph{reachable} from $s$).
A reachability certificate, namely any sequence $\rho$ of configurations
\[
q_0(\vr v_0) \step q_1(\vr v_1) \step \ldots \step q_n(\vr v_n)
\]
with $s = q_0(\vr v_0)$ and $t = q_n(\vr v_n)$,

% you try to actually parse the equation.

% why before fixing this.}
related by the step relation,
is said to be a \emph{run} from configuration $s$ to $t$.

\section{Outline of decidability proof}\label{sec:outline}

Consider a given symmetric 2-\cm $\mach = (Q,T)$ and 
two configurations, source $s$ and target $t$  ($s,t \in Q\times\N^2$).

\para{Decomposing a run into segments}
For convenience, we perform on $\mach$ the following notational
preprocessing:
we replace every zero-test transition
$t=(q,\ztinstr i, q')\in T$ by a sequence of two transitions,
\[
(q, \ztinstr i, q^t) \qquad
(q^t, \updinstr {0,0}, q'),
\]
% q \xstep{\textsf{c}_i=0?} S_i^t \xstep{(0,0)} q',\]
where $q^t$ is a new state, unique to the transition $t$.
Notice that reachability in the new symmetric 2-\cm thus obtained 
is equivalent to the original one, 
and that it has special states $q^t$
which can only be entered if the counter zero-tested by 
transition $t$ is zero;
we call them \emph{zero-testing states}.
The zero-testing states have the property
that they ``remember'' which counter is zero 
when the control of the machine is at them,
and that zero-test transitions are exactly those entering
a zero-testing state.
A fragment (infix) of a run between two consecutive zero-testing
states consists of only update transitions, except for the very last
transition which is a zero-test.
Such a fragment, called \emph{segment} in the sequel,
may be considered as a 2-\vass run
that ends with one counter 0, and which counter is 0 is
determined by the ending, zero-testing state.
Formally, we will consider a segment as a run in the
\emph{underlying} symmetric 2-\vass $\V$
obtained by replacing every zero-test transition 
$(q, \ztinstr i, q^t)$ by
the corresponding $(0,0)$ update one
$(q, \updinstr{0,0}, q^t)$.

\begin{remark} \label{rem:wlog}
Furthermore, \mywlog we may assume that one counter is 0
in both source and target configurations.
For instance, if the source vector is $v=(v_1,v_2)$ with $0<v_1\leq v_2$,
we replace it by $(0,v_2-v_1)$ and add one extra symmetric transition
with update $(v_1,v_1)$.
We thus assume, \mywlog, 
that the source and target states are zero-testing as well.
\end{remark}

\[ \rho=\rho_0 \rho_1 \dots \rho_n,\]
where 
each $\rho_i$ starts and ends at consecutive zero-testing states of 
$\rho$.
% ---%

Every $\rho_i$ is thus
a run in the underlying 2-\vass $\V$,
having one of the following forms:
\begin{alignat}{2} 
  \begin{aligned} \label{eq:4cases}
q(a,0) &\step^\ast q'(0,a') \qquad\qquad  & q(0,a) &\step^\ast q'(a',0)\\
q(0,a) &\step^\ast q'(0,a') \qquad\qquad  & q(a,0) &\step^\ast q'(a',0),
% \\

% q(a,0) &\step^\ast q'(a',0),

where the initial and final states $q$ and $q'$ are zero-testing states, and no zero-testing

Notice that we may write just
% \begin{equation*} %\label{eq:basictran}
$q(a) \step^* q'(a')$
% \end{equation*}
without specifying which of the four cases occurs,
since the (zero-testing) states $q$, $q'$ uniquely determine that.
We follow the notational shorthand %\eqref{eq:basictran} 
throughout and, instead of 
$\step^*$, which is a binary relation on $Q\times\N^2$,
%  \  \subseteq  (Q\times\N^2)^2$
we prefer to use 
a binary relation on $Q\times\N$, 
\[
\reachrel \ \ \subseteq \ (Q\times\N)^2,
\]
where $q(a)\reachrel q'(a')$ if and only if 
any of the four cases of \eqref{eq:4cases} holds, namely
the underlying 2-\vass $\V$ has a corresponding run that does not
visit zero-testing states except for the initial and final ones.
% Without loss of generality, we also assume 
% that both the source and target vector have at least one 0.
In view of Remark \ref{rem:wlog}, our task is to decide if 
$s\reachrel^* t$
for $s,t\in Q\times\N$,
that is whether a given pair $(s,t)$ belongs to the reflexive-transitive
closure of $\reachrel$.

\para{Three types of segments}
As we will see in \Cref{sec:targetpump}, the relation $\reachrel$
can be split into three disjoint areas with different properties,
delineated by a machine-dependent constant~$C$
(see \Cref{fig:areas}).
Thus, every segment $\rho_i \colon q(a) \reachrel q'(a')$, 
depending on the value of $a-a'$,
falls into one of three categories,
%split the relation $\step^\ast$ into three subrelations,
%which represent different kinds of \vass runs in $(Q\times\N^2)$:
denoted by $\stepp +$, $\stepp -$, and $\stepp 0$.
For a segment $q(a) \reachrel q'(a')$, we write:
\begin{equation}
% \left.
\begin{aligned}\label{eq:tran0-+}
& q(a) \stepp + q'(a'),\quad && 
\text{if\ \ $a' > a\,+C${}};\\
& q(a) \stepp - q'(a'),\quad && 
\text{if\ \ $a\, > a'+C${}};\\
& q(a) \stepp 0\, q'(a'),\quad && 
\text{if\ \ $|a-a'|\leq C $},
\end{aligned}
% \right\}
\end{equation}
% 
where the precise definition of the constant $C$ will be given in \Cref{sec:targetpump}.
The reachability relation can now be expressed as
\[
\reachrel^* \ = \ 
\big(\stepp 0 \ \cup \ \stepp - \ \cup \ \stepp + \big)^*,
\] 
an arbitrary composition
of the three sub-relations arbitrarily many times
(note that the composition assures that \emph{control states match}).

% \bigskip\noindent
Using this categorization 
we split the run $\rho$ into three consecutive \emph{phases} (each possibly empty): 

\begin{itemize}
\item $\Phi_1$, the \emph{prefix} of $\rho$ ending with the \emph{last} $\rho_i$ of the form $\stepp +$,
\item $\Phi_2$, an \emph{infix} of $\rho$ consisting only of $\rho_i$'s of the form $\stepp {0}$, 
\item $\Phi_3$, the remaining \emph{suffix} of $\rho$ starting 
with \emph{the first remaining} $\rho_i$ of the form $\stepp -$.
% (followed by other \vass segments of the forms $\stepp -$ and $\stepp 0$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Clearly, the composition of the three phases yields
the reachability relation, that is
\begin{align} \label{eq:reachrel}
\reachrel^* \ = \ \Phi_1 \circ \Phi_2 \circ \Phi_3,
\end{align}
for  the three relations defined by the following rational expressions:
\begin{align}
  \begin{aligned} \label{eq:regexp}
&\Phi_1 \ = \ ((\stepp 0 \ \cup \ \stepp -)^\ast \circ \stepp +)^\ast,\\
&\Phi_2 \ = \ \ \stepp 0^\ast,\\
&\Phi_3 \ = \ (\stepp - \circ \stepp {0}^\ast)^\ast.
  \end{aligned}
\end{align}

\para{Outline of decidability proof}

The proof uses \emph{semilinear sets}.
Any subset of $\N^d$ of the form
\[
% \lin b {\{p_1,\dots,p_n\}} = 
\setof{b + k_1 p_1 + \cdots + k_n p_n}{k_1, \dots, k_n \in \N},
\]
for $b, p_1, \dots, p_n \in \N^d$,
is called a
\emph{linear} set with base $b$ and periods $p_1, \dots, p_n$.
A \emph{semilinear} set is a finite union of linear sets in the same
dimension $d$.
We say $X \subseteq Q \times \N^d$ is semilinear 
if for every $q \in Q$, 
the set 
\(\{n \mid (q,n)\in X\} \subseteq \N^d\)
is semilinear.
Similarly, 
$R \subseteq (Q\times \N^d)^2$ is semilinear if for every $q,q' \in Q$,
the set
\( \{(n,n') \mid ((q,n),(q',n')) \in R \} \subseteq \N^{2d}\)
is semilinear.

\Cref{thm:main} is proved by showing that the relation $\Phi_2$, 
as well as
the image of relation $\Phi_1$ and inverse image of $\Phi_3$,
are all
\emph{effectively semilinear}, \ie they are 
semilinear sets whose descriptions are computable
given the underlying 2-\cm $\mach$ and source and target
$s, t\in Q\times\N$.
Importantly, we only need to consider semilinear sets and relations
in dimension $d=1$.


\begin{lemma} \label{lem:0star}
Given a symmetric 2-\cm $\mach$, the relation
$\Phi_2 \subseteq (Q \times \N)^2$ 
is effectively semilinear.
% The relation $\stepp {0}^*$ is effectively semilinear.
\end{lemma}

\section{Effective semilinearity for $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_3$:

For complexity considerations,
we denote by $\mysize \mach$ the size of unary representation of $\mach$,

\para{Target and source pumping}

The \emph{target pumping} 
property of segments of the form $\stepp +$ is 
a central technical tool in the proof of
\Cref{lem:compbackfor}:

\newcommand{\punkt}[2]{({#1}{\rightarrow}{#2})}
\begin{lemma} [Target pumping] \label{lem:targetpump}
Given a symmetric 2-\vass $\V$,
one can compute numbers $C, D \in \N$ 
such that for every pair of control states $q, q'$ of $\V$ and  
$a, a'\in\N$ such that $a' > a + C$,%
% \[
% \punkt 1 2 \qquad
% \text{if} \quad q(a,0)\step^* q'(0,a') \quad \text{then} \quad q(a,0) \step^* q'(0, a'+D),
% \]
% and similarly for the other three symmetric cases:
\begin{alignat*}{4}
&\punkt 1 2 \qquad
\text{if} \quad q(a,0)\step^* q'(0,a') \quad &&\text{then} \quad q(a,0) \step^* q'(0, a'+D),\\
&\punkt 2 2 \qquad
\text{if} \quad q(0,a)\step^* q'(0,a') \quad &&\text{then}  \quad  q(0,a) \step^* q'(0, a'+D),\\
&\punkt 2 1 \qquad
\text{if} \quad q(0,a)\step^* q'(a',0) \quad &&\text{then}  \quad  q(0,a) \step^* q'(a'+D, 0),\\
&\punkt 1 1 \qquad
\text{if} \quad q(a,0)\step^* q'(a',0) \quad &&\text{then}  \quad  q(a,0) \step^* q'(a'+D,0).
\end{alignat*}
Moreover, $C$ is polynomial in $\mysize \V$ and $D$ is exponential in $\mysize \V$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}
It is worth noting that the proof of \Cref{lem:targetpump},
to be given in \Cref{sec:targetpump}, is the part of
our development that unavoidably relies on symmetry.
\Cref{lem:targetpump} fails for non-symmetric 2-\vass,
for instance point $\punkt 1 2$ fails 
for a 2-\vass with one state $q$ and one transition $(q,(-1,2),q)$.
% \Cref{lem:targetpump} is a cornerstone of the proof
% of \Cref{lem:compbackfor}.
% \mei{We already said this just above the statement
% of the lemma, let's not repeat ourselves}
\end{remark}

We apply \Cref{lem:targetpump} to the underlying 
symmetric 2-\vass $\V$ of a given symmetric 2-\cm $\mach$.
The four claims of \Cref{lem:targetpump} jointly imply that 
$\stepp +$ is \emph{target-pumpable}, 
that is for every pair of zero-testing control states $q, q'$,
% and  $a, a'\in\N$ such that $a, a'-a > C$, 
\begin{align} \label{eq:tpump}
q(a)\stepp + q'(a') \quad \implies  \quad  q(a) \stepp + q'(a'+D).
\end{align}
The reverse version of the four claims of \Cref{lem:targetpump}, 
where we swap source and target and reverse all transitions
(the reverse of $(q,w,q')$ is $(q',-w,q)$, as expected), 
implies that $\stepp -$ is \emph{source-pumpable}, that is 
\begin{align} \label{eq:spump}
q(a)\stepp - q'(a') \quad \implies  \quad  q(a+D) \stepp - q'(a').
\end{align}
Note that we can assume the same $D$ in \eqref{eq:tpump} and 
\eqref{eq:spump}, by taking the least common multiple of the
two constants given by the two applications of \Cref{lem:targetpump},
and also the same $C$, by taking the maximum of the two constants.
In the sequel, fix the constant 
$D$ from \eqref{eq:tpump} and \eqref{eq:spump}.


\para{\Saturated sets}

It is folklore that semilinear sets $X\subseteq Q\times\N$
are exactly \emph{ultimately periodic} sets, namely satisfying
$q(a)\in X\iff q(a+N) \in X$ for some $N>0,M\geq 0$, all $q\in Q$ 
and all sufficiently large $a\geq M$.
We call $N$ and $M$ a \emph{period} and \emph{threshold} of $X$.

Given $N>0$, a subset $X\subseteq Q\times\N$ we call  \emph{$N$-\saturated} 
if for all $(q,a) \in X$, also $(q,a+N) \in X$.
% if $(q,a) \in X \implies (q,a+N) \in X$ for all $q\in Q$ and $a\in\N$.
$N$-\saturated sets
are ultimately periodic, and hence semilinear.
% 
An $N$-\saturated set $X$ is representable by a function 
$\minf X : Q\times \setfromto 0 {N-1} \to \N \cup \{\infty\}$ 
that maps a state $q\in Q$ and $r<N$ to the least number $n$ 
whose remainder modulo $N$ equals $r$ and  $(q,n)\in X$,
\[
\minf X (q,r) \ = \ \min\{n : (q,n) \in X, \ \ n \equiv r \bmod N\},
\]
and to $\infty$ if there is no such number $n$.
We call $N$ a \emph{period} of $X$, and the numbers 
$\setof{\minf X(q,r)}{\minf X(q,r)\in\N}$  its \emph{bases}.
% 
For instance, in consequence of \eqref{eq:tpump} and \eqref{eq:spump}, 
the image $S\circ \stepp +$ as well as the inverse image
$\stepp - \circ\, S$ is always $D$-\saturated, irrespectively of
$S\subseteq Q\times\N$.

For termination of our decision procedure it will be crucial that
the semilinear sets it maintains form
non-decreasing chains 
of $D$-\saturated sets $X\subseteq Q\times\N$, since we have:
%  the following lemma:
\begin{lemma} \label{lem:stab}
    For every $N\in\N$, 
    every non-decreasing chain of $X_1 \subseteq X_2 \subseteq \cdots$ of $N$-\saturated subsets of $Q\times\N$ stabilizes.
\end{lemma}
% 
\begin{proof}
The sequence $\minf {X_1}, \minf{X_2}, \ldots$ is a non-increasing
chain of functions, ordered pointwise 
(assuming $n<\infty$ for $n\in\N$), 
and hence necessarily stabilizes due to well-foundedness
of the function space $Q\times \setfromto 0 {N-1} \to \N \cup \{\infty\}$.
\end{proof}


Before the proofs of \Cref{lem:0star,lem:compbackfor},
a basic observation concerning individual segments:

\begin{lemma} \label{lem:0-+}
The relations $\stepp 0, \ \stepp -, \ \stepp +$ are effectively semilinear.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The reachability relation of the underlying 2-\vass $\V$
is effectively semilinear \cite{Blondin15},
which implies that so is $\reachrel$, the restriction thereof 
enforcing a counter to be 0 initially or finally, 
and forbidding zero-testing states except the source and target ones.
In consequence, its further Presburger-definable restrictions 
$\stepp 0, \ \stepp -, \ \stepp +$ are also effectively semilinear.
\end{proof}


\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:compbackfor}]
Recall that $\Phi_3 \ = \ (\stepp - \circ \stepp {0}^\ast)^\ast$.
By \Cref{lem:0-+,lem:0star}, both the relations $\stepp -$ and
$(\stepp 0)^*$ are effectively semilinear.
Using the effective equivalence of semilinear sets and Presburger Arithmetic,
the composed relation 
$\stepp - \circ \stepp {0}^\ast$ is also effectively semilinear,
as well as 
$(\stepp - \circ \stepp {0}^\ast) \circ S$ for every given 
semilinear set $S$.
Furthermore, as $\stepp -$ is source pumpable \eqref{eq:spump},
and therefore the composed relation $\stepp - \circ \stepp {0}^\ast$
is so as well, the set
$(\stepp - \circ \stepp {0}^\ast) \circ S$ is forcedly $D$-\saturated.

In order to  argue that the set $\Phi_3 \circ \{t\}$
is effectively semilinear we make use of \Cref{lem:stab},
iterating the transformation
\[
X_{i+1} \ := \ X_{i} \ \cup \ (\stepp - \circ \stepp {0}^\ast) \circ X_{i},
\]
starting from the initial set
\[
X_0 \ := \ (\stepp - \circ \stepp {0}^\ast) \circ \{t\},
\]
until a fixpoint is reached. By the above observations, we deduce that
both the initial set $X_0$, as well as every successive iterate $X_{i+1}$,
are effectively semilinear and $D$-\saturated; moreover it is
self-evident that the sequence $X_0 \subseteq X_1 \subseteq \ldots$
is non-decreasing.

More explicitly, by \Cref{lem:stab} we have that at starting at some $j$,
\[
X_j \ = \ X_{j+1} \ = \ X_{j+2} \ = \ \ldots
\] 
Since both the iteration
$X_i \mapsto X_{i+1}$ and the equality test $X_i = X_{i+1}$ are effective,
the set 
\[
\Phi_3\circ \{t\} \ = \ \{t\} \ \cup \ \bigcup_{i\in\N} X_i \ = \ 
\{t\} \ \cup \ X_j
\]
is effectively semilinear.
We conclude the first part by 
noting that the proof is not specific to the starting set $\{t\}$,
and shows that $\Phi_3 \circ S$ is effectively 
semilinear given any semilinear $S\subseteq Q\times\N$,
which will be useful in the second part.

\medskip

Now we proceed to arguing that
the set $\{s\} \circ \Phi_1$ is effectively semilinear.
To this aim we prefer to use another rational expression defining 
$\Phi_1$, where $\Phi_3$ appears as a subexpression:
\[
\Phi_1 \ = \ (\stepp{0}^* \ \circ \ \Phi_3 \ \circ \ \stepp +)^\ast;
% \qquad\qquad\qquad
% \text{recalling } \Phi_3 \ = \ (\stepp - \circ \stepp {0}^\ast)^\ast;
\]
indeed, as in \eqref{eq:regexp} in \Cref{sec:outline}, the inner subexpression $\stepp{0}^*  \circ \  \Phi_3  \  \circ  \stepp +$ 
describes an arbitrary
composition of segments of type $\stepp 0$ and $\stepp -$ that ends 
with one segment of type $\stepp +$.

The structure of the proof is similar to the previous part, namely
we proceed by iterating the relation defined by the inner subexpression, 
that is we consider the initial set
and the consecutive iterates:
\[
X_0 \ := \ f\bigl(\{s\}\bigr) 
\qquad\qquad\qquad
% \ \circ \ \bigl((\stepp 0)^* \ \circ \ \Phi_3 \ \circ \ \stepp +\bigr)
% \]
% \[
X_{i+1} \ := \ X_i \ \ \cup \ \ 
f\bigl(X_i\bigr)
%  \  \circ \  \bigl((\stepp 0)^* \ \circ \ \Phi_3 \ \circ \ \stepp +\bigr).
\]
where
\[
f(S) \ = \ S \ 
\circ \ \bigl(\stepp{0}^* \ \circ \ \Phi_3 \ \circ \ \stepp +\bigr).
\]
Similarly as before, knowing that $\stepp +$ is target pumpable, we know
that all $X_i$ are $D$-\saturated sets, and therefore the sequence 
$X_0, X_1, \ldots$ stabilizes, and hence
\[
\{s\} \circ  \Phi_1 \ = \ 
 \{s\} \ \cup \  X_j
\]
for some $j$, similarly as before.
The only missing ingredient is computability of the iteration
$X_i \mapsto X_{i+1}$.
However, in order to apply the previously shown computability of 
the function
\begin{align} \label{eq:back}
S \ \mapsto \ \Phi_3 \ \circ \ S
\end{align}
which proceeds 'backward', for computability
of the function
$
S \, \mapsto  f(S)
%  \ := \ S \ 
% \circ \ \bigl((\stepp 0)^* \ \circ \ \Phi_3 \ \circ \ \stepp +\bigr)
$
which proceeds 'forward', we need a trick.
Note that the relation 
$(\stepp 0)^* \ \circ \ \Phi_3 \ \circ \ \stepp +$ is always
target-pumpable since $\stepp +$ is so \eqref{eq:tpump},
and hence $f(S)$ is always $D$-\saturated
irrespectively of $S$.
To compute $f(S)$, one first establishes, for every state $q\in Q$, 
which remainders $r$ modulo $D$ appear in this set,
by applying the transformation \eqref{eq:back} to the set
\begin{align} \label{eq:set1}
\bigl(\stepp + \circ \ X_{q,r}\bigr)
,
\end{align} 
% where $M_r = r + D\cdot\N$ contains all numbers congruent to $r$ modulo $D$, 
where $X_{q,r} = \setof{q(n)}{n\equiv r \bmod D}$,
and checking nonemptiness of intersection with 
the set 
\begin{align} \label{eq:set2}
S\ \,\circ \ \stepp{0}^*.
\end{align} 
Both the sets \eqref{eq:set1}, \eqref{eq:set2} 
are effectively semilinear due to 
\Cref{lem:0-+,lem:0star}, and hence the set
$\Phi_3 \ \circ \ \bigl(\stepp + \circ \ X_{q,r}\bigr)$,
and its intersection $Y_{q,r}$ with \eqref{eq:set2}, are
computable.
Then one finds the representation
$\minf{f(S)}$ of $f(S)$ by an \emph{exhaustive search}.
For every positively verified state-remainder pair $(q,r)$,
namely one with $Y_{q,r}\neq\emptyset$, 
one finds the least number $n$ such that
$q(n)\in f(S)$ and $n\equiv r \bmod D$ by
an exhaustive search:
for consecutive candidates $k=0, 1, \ldots$, 
apply the transformation \eqref{eq:back} to the set
\[
\bigl(\stepp + \circ \ \{q(r+k\cdot D)\}\bigr)
\]
and check nonemptiness of intersection with the set \eqref{eq:set2},
until nonempty intersection is found.
This concludes the proof of effective $D$-\saturation
of $f(S)$, and thus also the proof of the second part of \Cref{lem:compbackfor}.
\end{proof}

    \[
        \setof{ a^n b^{n'} }{ q[n \bmod N](n) \step_{\mach'}^* q'[n' \bmod N](n') }.
