\begin{abstract}
Assuming effective semilinearity of the full reachability relations of
symmetric two-counter machines and symmetric three-dimensional VASS,
we give a construction for deciding reachability in symmetric
three-counter machines. The construction combines a fixed connector
relation with independent grid pumps. Its abstract saturation procedure
requires only box-to-box emptiness for the connector relation.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Research status.}
This is a LaTeX transcription of the assembled research proof.
The abstract completion theorem, normalization, box oracle, private-word
ports, modulus bookkeeping, and scalar bounded-band compiler were
independently reviewed in the accompanying research process.
It is not a machine-checked formalization. Detailed supporting constructions
are retained in the research log referenced at the end; this document
does not reproduce all those supporting notes.
No effective-semilinearity conclusion for the full three-counter
reachability relation is asserted.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Assume the following two algorithms.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.8em,style=nextline]
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{S2}}] Given a symmetric two-counter machine,
compute a Presburger formula for its full reachability relation.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{S3}}] Given a symmetric three-dimensional VASS,
compute a Presburger formula for its full reachability relation.
\end{description}
Then point-to-point reachability in a symmetric three-counter machine
is decidable.
\end{theorem}


Here symmetry means closure of every update transition under permutations of its counter coordinates, preserving its source and target control states. Zero tests name their tested counter. The construction does not assume that transitions are reversible. Reversal is used to construct and analyze a different machine, which remains symmetric.

The proof uses independent grid pumps and a box-query oracle. It does not require semilinearity of unrestricted three-counter reachability, nor semilinearity of the closure of all bounded-gain boundary segments.

\paragraph{Notation.}
We write PA for Presburger-definable. Relations are composed from left to
right: \(x(R;S)z\) means that \(xRy\) and \(ySz\) for some \(y\).
The symbol \(R^*\) denotes reflexive-transitive closure. For a set \(S\),
\(S;R\) is its forward image, and \(R^{-1}(T)\) is the preimage of \(T\).

\section{An abstract completion theorem}\label{sec:completion}

Let \(X\) be a finite disjoint union of charts \(\{q\}\times\N^{d(q)}\), with dimensions at most three. Fix a positive modulus \(D\). A grid box is a product of intervals in fixed coordinatewise residues modulo \(D\), where each interval has a finite lower bound and may have a finite or infinite upper bound. Let \(\mathcal B\) be their finite-union Boolean algebra. The order within a chart and residue is componentwise increase by \(D\N^{d(q)}\).

Let \(K\) be a \emph{fixed} relation on \(X\). It need not be transitive or semilinear. Suppose the question

\begin{equation}
K(S,T)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists x\in S\ \exists y\in T:\ xKy. \label{eq:box-query}
\end{equation}


is decidable for every \(S,T\) in \(\mathcal B\).

At selected eligible charts let \(U\) independently add arbitrary multiples of \(D\) to the coordinates. Eligibility is a grid upset. At other selected charts let \(V\) independently subtract multiples of \(D\), keeping both endpoints eligible. Both events allow zero addition/subtraction. An event may change an input chart to a designated output chart of the same dimension. Dimension zero is allowed.

\begin{theorem}[Grid-pump completion]\label{thm:completion}

Then the relation

\begin{equation}
L=K;\bigl((U\cup V);K\bigr)^*. \label{eq:language}
\end{equation}


has decidable box-to-box emptiness.

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

Box sets are effectively closed under Boolean operations, \(U\)-preimages and \(V\)-images. A grid-upward set has a finite basis by Dickson's lemma. Moreover an intersection oracle for an upward set \(Y\) against all boxes computes its basis: maintain a finite upward approximation \(A\); test whether \(Y\cap(X\setminus A)\) is empty; if it is nonempty, enumerate singleton witnesses outside \(A\) and add one generated upset. Each enlargement is strict, so the ascending-chain condition for upward subsets of a well-quasi-order terminates the procedure. The oracle gives the effective stopping test.

Put \(\mathcal C=(V;K)^*\). For a box \(T\) iterate

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}T_0&=T,\\ T_{n+1}&=T\cup V^{-1}\bigl(K^{-1}(T_n)\bigr).\end{aligned}\label{eq:backward}
\end{equation}


The added part \(Y_n\) is upward. Its intersection with a box \(E\) is decided by the available query

\begin{equation*}
K\bigl(V(E),T_n\bigr).
\end{equation*}


Thus \(Y_n\) is effectively computed by the basis procedure. The sequence consists of the fixed \(T\) together with an increasing upward part, so it stabilizes effectively at \(\mathcal C^{-1}(T)\).

For a box \(S\) put \(f(S)=S;K;\mathcal C;U\). This is upward, and

\begin{equation}
f(S)\cap E\neq\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\bigl(S,\mathcal C^{-1}(U^{-1}(E))\bigr). \label{eq:forward-query}
\end{equation}


Again the basis procedure computes \(f(S)\). Iterating \(S_{n+1}=S_0\cup f(S_n)\) stabilizes at \(S_0;\mathcal A\), where \(\mathcal A=(K;\mathcal C;U)^*\). Finally

\begin{equation}
L=\mathcal A;K;\mathcal C. \label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}


This identity follows by splitting an alternating word immediately after each \(U\). It does not use transitivity of \(K\). The final question is the available query \(K(S_0;\mathcal A,\mathcal C^{-1}(T))\). This proves the theorem.

\end{proof}

\paragraph{Private interfaces.}

Two interface conventions will be needed. A Cartesian relation \(E\times F\) can be exposed as an event at a zero-dimensional private port: check \(E\) before the event and generate \(F\) after it. A right-saturated relation \(R\) with \(R;U=R\) can be factored through a private \(U\) event, provided \emph{every} entrance into that event passes through \(R\). Soundness then follows by absorbing \(R;U\) into \(R\). Dually use \(V;R=R\). Such a private event is not asserted to be a physical self-loop.

\section{Geometric and lower-dimensional ingredients}\label{sec:ingredients}

The following are proved supporting lemmas, rather than extra assumptions. Their detailed constructions and audits are retained in the accompanying research log and the named proof notes.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*]

\item\label{item:ingredient-a} A fixed bounded-minimum region of a symmetric three-counter machine has an exact symmetric two-counter presentation. Store a chosen bounded physical coordinate and its value in finite control and keep the other two values as counters. Away from a region with two bounded coordinates, the chosen physical coordinate cannot change: a change within one update, whose size is bounded, forces two bounded coordinates. Tests of the stored coordinate are finite-control guards; any test of a free coordinate is a genuine scalar-axis visit. Finite change-of-marker gadgets at those visits preserve the exact physical endpoint values. Consequently \textbf{S2} supplies the reachability formulas needed for this fixed thin subsystem. Helper states representing totals or erasing counters are never mistaken for physical axes.

\item\label{item:ingredient-b} In a strongly connected symmetric update graph, a non-diagonal pointed cycle cone is either positive or negative: all its nonzero cycle vectors have strictly positive total, or all have strictly negative total. A nonzero zero-total orbit or opposite total signs would create lineality. Non-diagonal nonpointed components have the previously constructed exact transfer-anchor presentation: high vectors with the same total and appropriate coordinate residues are mutually reachable. Its scalar port records that total and residues. Diagonal low-to-low paths admit an actual uniformly thin replacement. Diagonal connectors incident to true high transfer ports have exact lower-dimensional presentations. They remain explicit hub/base connectors, not arbitrary relations inserted between productive cores.

\item\label{item:ingredient-c} A positive pointed component is productive when its cone contains a vector with a negative coordinate. For every \(i\), the face diagonal \(d_i\) (zero in coordinate \(i\) and one in the other two) lies in the full interior of that cone. Indeed, from a negative-coordinate vector and its free swap obtain \((-a,b,b)\), where \(2b>a>0\). Its three permutations are independent and a strictly positive combination equals a positive multiple of \(d_i\). The negative statement is reversed.

\item\label{item:ingredient-d} The high-endpoint cone/lattice lemma supplies a uniform threshold \(H\), a full-rank lattice \(\Lambda\), and a fixed correction vector \(k\) such that suitable high endpoints \(u,v\) are joined whenever \(v-u\) is in the correct path lattice coset and \(v-u-k\) lies in the cycle cone. The proof uses all simple-cycle orbits, an affine-semigroup conductor, finite connector words, and bounded-error ordering of loops at a common control state. It does not assume that cycles at different states can be freely reordered. Boundary access is supplied by finite actual ingress/egress word catalogs: the access domains are upward Presburger sets by \textbf{S3}, hence have computable finite bases.

For a productive positive core, (c),(d) give a full-rank target period monoid on every bounded-coordinate face, with the balanced free direction in its interior. They also give input lifting: increasing the free source coordinates can be compensated by moving sufficiently far in that target face cone. Thus the marked productive face relation is weakly monotone on a sufficiently divisible grid. Its preimage of a grid-upward set is upward and effectively Presburger by \textbf{S3}. Repeated backward preimages stabilize by Dickson's lemma.

\item\label{item:ingredient-e} If all cycles of a positive component are coordinatewise nonnegative, coordinate potentials show that any path ending with coordinate \(i\) bounded has that coordinate bounded throughout, by the endpoint bound plus a machine constant. The negative counterpart applies at the source. Across a finite condensation DAG the constants add. These orthant components provide thin replacements and the endpoint-box exceptions discussed below.

\item\label{item:ingredient-f} Finite patterns of symmetric three-VASS pieces, exact symmetric two-CM pieces, and fixed tests/words have effectively semilinear relations by \textbf{S2} and \textbf{S3}. For prescribed scalar-axis endpoints they also have effective target pumping when target total minus source total is sufficiently large, and source pumping when it is sufficiently negative. The proof takes semilinear tuples of VASS schemes and scalar two-CM middle relations, chooses a positive-gain global period, and uses the water-filling offset

\begin{equation*}
w(x)=x-\proj_{\{z\geq 0:\,\sum_i z_i\leq\alpha\}}(x),
\end{equation*}


where \(\alpha\) is the source total increment of that period. This offset vanishes at the source and is a positive axis increment at the target. VASS cycles are inserted in permitted orientations; full two-CM stages are handled through their scalar pumping theorem, without a false finite-flat-cover claim for counter machines. Bounded physical coordinates have homogeneous increment zero. The construction is effective by the semilinear presentations and finite witness enumeration.

For the positive patterns used below, gain filters and recorded high-core visits are preserved: inserted cycles have nonnegative total, so a fat gain cannot decrease below its cutoff; old high-core configurations survive with nonnegative offsets. Wide two-counter stages are translated by a fixed lower-bound vector before applying the argument. Reverse for negative patterns. This extension is not asserted for arbitrary filters on a full two-CM relation.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Residual pair coercivity}\label{sec:pair}

For an update \(\delta\) put \(t(\delta)=\sum_i\delta_i\) and let \(a(\delta)\) be the sum of its two smallest entries. If a positive symmetric component has a coordinate axis in the interior of its cone, there are actual independent axis-pump words at every state; expose them as full three-dimensional grid events.

Otherwise every cycle has nonnegative \(a\)-weight. Choose a shortest-path potential for \(a\). In the zero-reduced-\(a\) graph mark every vertex of an SCC supporting a positive-total cycle. A closed word there can be oriented to have zero effect on a fixed coordinate pair in total and strictly positive effect on its complement. Concatenating with the swap of that pair gives an actual pure-axis pump. Full symmetry gives the other axes. Expose high visits as grid events; low visits belong to the fixed thin subsystem.

After deleting marked controls, every positive-total cycle has strictly positive \(a\)-weight. Hence a rational \(\varepsilon>0\) and a finite potential give, for every residual path \(x\to y\),

\begin{equation}
s_2(y)-s_2(x)\geq \varepsilon\bigl(\tot y-\tot x\bigr)-R, \label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}


where \(s_2\) is the sum of the two smallest coordinates. The same estimate holds for each fixed physical coordinate pair. It follows by summing \(a(\delta)-\varepsilon t(\delta)\), using

\begin{equation*}
s_2(x+\delta)\geq s_2(x)+a(\delta).
\end{equation*}


Let \(V_0\) be the fixed outer face modes, including native thin-simulator modes but excluding later private word interiors. Keep the original fat-interface collar distinct from any larger finite marker values inside boundary catalogs or the thin simulator: enlarging the latter does not redefine the former or restart its construction. At mode \(v\) store a bounded marker \(b_v\) and a free pair \(z\). Put

\begin{equation*}
f_v(z)=b_v+\min(z_1,z_2).
\end{equation*}


If \(B\) bounds these outer marker values, then \(0\leq f_v-s_2\leq B\). For an ordinary fixed-marker word from \(b\) to \(b'\) with free effect \((u,v)\), its guaranteed \(f\)-gain is

\begin{equation}
w_2=\min(u,v)+b'-b. \label{eq:ordinary-weight}
\end{equation}


Fix a large gain cutoff \(\Co\). Only \emph{positive} same-marker pieces of gain at most \(\Co\) are compiled into the positive ordinary graph; use a separate reversed construction for negative pieces. Nonnegative reduced total costs bound their word lengths, after deleting zero-effect cycles. If a high-core visit must be retained, split there first and shorten each half separately. All lower total and pair losses are bounded independently of \(\Co\). A bounded-gain cross-marker piece has uniformly bounded coordinate displacement by the same reduced-cost estimate: the source marker and the target marker are both bounded at both endpoints. Choose \(W\) later above this bound and treat these pieces as scalar-axis endpoint patterns. Thus every remaining residual fat edge really has gain greater than \(\Co\).

Every finite word catalog is closed under \emph{all} independent free-coordinate swaps at its individual steps. Each complete oriented word is an outer edge. This is necessary for an exact symmetric two-VASS chain presentation; whole-word swaps alone would be insufficient. All expansions retain the physical total and marker trace, and \eqref{eq:pair} supplies the same cutoff-independent pair-loss bound.

An ordinary closed word can be oriented to have free effect \((A,T-A)\), where \(A\) is its accumulated \(w_2\) and \(T\) its total. If \(A<0\), then \(T<0\) yields a negative free diagonal after concatenating the free swap; \(T=0\) yields an actual transfer hub; \(T>0\) yields independent positive free-axis pumps. \(A=0,\ T>0\) also yields independent pumps. All have finite actual word catalogs and enabling buffers.

After these marked cases are dealt with by Section~\ref{sec:normal}, surviving ordinary cycles have nonnegative pair weight. Let \(-P_2\) bound each ordinary edge's weight. Choose \(\Co\) so that

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}\Gamma&=\varepsilon\Co-R-B,\\ \Gamma-1&>(|V_0|-1)P_2.\end{aligned}\label{eq:cutoff}
\end{equation}


Give each fat edge formal weight \(\Gamma-1\). Every cycle of this finite formal graph is nonnegative: delete ordinary cycles between fat edges and use \eqref{eq:cutoff}. A potential \(h_2\) therefore makes \(f_v+h_2(v)\) nondecreasing, with an increase of at least one per fat edge. A positive phase ending at a bounded physical pair has a computable uniform bound on its number of fat edges. The bound is independent of its unbounded surviving coordinate. Negative phases have the reversed source-bound property.

Only after \(\Co\) and the finite catalogs are fixed choose the wide/axis threshold \(W\). If an axis cut lies inside a private word, keep its word-position and mandatory-continuation tag. Such scalar ports are endpoint adapters, not new vertices of \(V_0\). A fixed maximum prefix displacement \(A\) changes the endpoint pair bound to at most \(B+W+A\). Thus these cuts do not create circular constants. A later scalar pumping cutoff \(\Cs\) and common modulus \(D\) do not change \(\Co\) or \(W\). Potentials on \(V_0\) lift unchanged to residue refinements. Hub canonical bounded coordinates retain the \emph{original} transfer modulus; refining \(D\) changes the scalar-total residue classes, not the sizes of those bounded representatives. Repeating actual nonnegative or nonpositive coordinate pump words to obtain \(D\)-multiple effects needs no larger enabling threshold when both event endpoints remain eligible.

\section{One fixed normal-form language}\label{sec:normal}

We now describe the exact normalization rather than assuming that finitely many semilinear macro relations have a decidable closure.

Productive high occurrences have signs \(P,N\). Between physical scalar-axis cuts, every actual thin corridor has a fixed bounded physical marker. A \(P\)-to-\(N\) peak across such a corridor has an exact finite union of Cartesian source-domain \(\times\) target-domain replacements: finite residue ingress/egress catalogs and a positive face-interior cone followed by the opposite diagonal pump connect every pair in the chosen product. Expose each product as a zero-dimensional event.

An \(N\)-to-\(P\) valley has an actual replacement with a physical axis cut. Select the \emph{last} negative productive SCC with an actual high visit and the \emph{first} positive productive SCC with such a visit. Freeze everything outside these two core occurrences. Shallow intervening productive pieces are thin; intervening nonproductive pieces have orthant coordinate potentials. Outside an existing axis cut their bounded-marker identity is fixed, say \(i\). If \(M\) is the least free-coordinate value over the genuine thin middle, all adjacent orthant tails have free values at least \(M-R_0\). Subtract a sufficiently divisible multiple of \(d_i\) from this middle until one free coordinate is bounded. Its marker and all its tests are unchanged. Reconstruct only the selected single-SCC core pieces: their facet deficits are uniformly bounded, and the signed interior directions \(-d_i,+d_i\) repair them. Low-marker SCC exits use finite boundary catalogs. No claim is made that a whole multi-SCC displacement belongs to one core's cone.

The same valley/peak replacements hold with one side replaced by an available ordinary free-diagonal loop. These are the replacements needed for ordinary contrary-sign cycles.

Termination is explicit. Before choosing \(\Co\) label the original finite list of deep productive occurrences. A peak consumes origins; a valley separates unlike adjacent origins by an axis cut, retaining their signs. The lexicographic pair (retained origins, unlike-sign adjacencies in uncut intervals) decreases. Reconstructed signed pieces are atomic relations in their \emph{same} residual signed graphs; they are not recursively expanded at every new physical collar visit.

After the sign-specific small-word catalogs are fixed, label ordinary adverse wide visits. In a positive interval, a negative-diagonal visit after a fat is removed with the \emph{last} preceding fat by a one-fat peak, decreasing the number of fat origins. If all such visits precede the first fat, choose the \emph{last} one and perform a one-fat valley; the selected visit and inserted negative loops lie in the base-only prefix before the new axis cut. Thus (retained fat origins, original adverse visits in still-fat-containing intervals) strictly decreases. The negative construction is reversed. New private words are confined to base-only pieces, atomic signed relations, or mandatory adapters; they are not added as unrestricted edges of the residual ordinary graph.

Grid visits are exposed as actual independent-pump events. A genuine high transfer visit \emph{after} a positive fat is handled by its last-fat-to-hub relation \(R\) followed by a private scalar \(\Up\). Here \(R\) is PA by \textbf{S2} and \textbf{S3} and \(R;\Up=R\): pump a positive face period at the last core and translate the following fixed-marker corridor. The reverse is a private \(\Down\) \emph{before} a hub-to-negative-fat relation. These requirements are mandatory; in particular a negative scalar phase cannot begin by freely expanding a canonical hub into arbitrary high representatives and then use a fictitious bounded source pair.

A hub before the first positive fat may be represented by a base-only detour to its canonical scalar representative and the exact inverse hub adapter. The latter exposes every high representative with the same total/residues. The positive residual pair count begins after this adapter; no earlier high pair is bounded by its canonical representative. Reverse for a negative phase. The same original-visit measure terminates these hub cuts.

Initial/final productive occurrences are included in the sign normalization, with exterior endpoints frozen. Orthant endpoint exceptions are retained explicitly: a negative-orthant initial fragment maps a source box to an upward face set or a PA bounded-coordinate strip; a positive-orthant initial fragment ending on a face is uniformly thin. Reverse for terminal fragments. An arbitrary mixed-sign \textbf{S3} relation is \emph{not} silently prepended to a negative phase.

All modes and relation types are fixed from the machine: physical faces, full three-dimensional pump interfaces, physical scalar axes (including private-word continuation tags), true total/residue hubs, Cartesian reset ports, and mandatory private scalar pump ports. Erase origin labels after the termination proof. Let \(K\) be the resulting fixed connector language between designated events, allowing arbitrary composition only through its scalar-axis interfaces and exact lower subsystem. This is a fixed sound normalized relation, not a query-dependent family and not the unrestricted reachability relation in disguise.

Soundness follows from actual word/macro realizations, true hub equivalence, Cartesian reset soundness, and absorption of private \(R;\Up\) or \(\Down;R\). Completeness is the terminating normalization just proved. Hence original reachability between original endpoint charts equals \eqref{eq:language}.

\section[Deciding K between boxes]{Deciding \(K\) between boxes}\label{sec:oracle}

First consider one positive residual face-to-face phase.

If the target box has both coordinates unbounded above, it is grid-upward. The weakly monotone PA face transitions from Section~\ref{sec:ingredients}(d) permit effective backward upward saturation. Any PA source set can then be tested against the computed PA predecessor.

If the target box bounds at least one free coordinate, its physical pair is bounded. Section~\ref{sec:pair} bounds the number of fat edges. Enumerate the finitely many patterns up to that bound; ordinary corridors are exact symmetric two-VASS relations, fat stages are \textbf{S3} relations, and fixed adapters are explicit. \textbf{S2}, \textbf{S3}, and PA composition decide the query and compute its restricted relation. Negative phases are handled by reversing the whole query.

For full three-dimensional target boxes, a fully unbounded box gives an upward ingress domain by translation. If exactly one coordinate \(i\) is bounded, split its finite interval. At a productive residual core, \(d_i\) is interior to its cone: translate the prefix by an arbitrary source increment and the suffix by a sufficiently large multiple of \(d_i\), then splice at the high core using the cone/lattice lemma. This yields an upward PA face source domain for that branch. In the orthant branch a coordinate potential bounds the corresponding source coordinate; if it is free, this is a bounded-pair PA strip, and if it is the marker, free translation gives upwardness. Two bounded target coordinates give a bounded-pair preimage by the residual pair inequality. \textbf{S3} computes the exact ingress relations; no semantically punctured graph is substituted for a symmetric VASS. Reverse all signs and endpoint roles for negative phases.

For a last-positive-fat hub adapter \(R\), an unbounded scalar target tail has an upward PA source domain: use the cofinal positive face periods to enable a finite residue-compatible corridor word, then pump beyond the target threshold. A bounded scalar target has finitely many possible source vectors, by the positive proper-total potential. Thus the preceding positive phase is queried against an upward set or a bounded-pair set. A scalar hub source tail denotes an upward PA family of high representatives; a bounded scalar source denotes finitely many representatives. The negative cases are dual and obey the mandatory-event grammar in Section~\ref{sec:normal}.

Private reset adapters are finite unions of grid-upward products. Fixed partial-word adapters preserve boxes by translations and finite enabling bounds. PA bounded-coordinate strips are handled by bounded-pattern enumeration; alternatively they decompose into finitely many unary semilinear slices. Internal calculations may use a query-dependent modulus \(M\) divisible by the fixed structural periods and \(D\). They need not force every resulting PA congruence to divide the \emph{outer} saturation period \(D\): \(K\) returns only a Boolean, and its internal refinement changes no transitions or thresholds. The outer upward sets are \(D\)-upward because of the actual events, independently of these internal calculations.

It remains to allow arbitrarily many \emph{internal} physical axis visits. Compute the unary PA first-axis set \(I\) reachable from the source boxes without an intervening chosen scalar-axis port, and the unary PA last-axis set \(J\) from which the target boxes are reachable without another one. The preceding cases compute these sets: bounded pairs give finite PA patterns, while upward cases give effective PA predecessors.

Between consecutive scalar-axis ports, positive residual patterns have uniformly bounded fat count from their terminal pair; negative patterns have the source bound. Exact fixed-value zeroizers reduce their collar endpoints to literal axes for Section~\ref{sec:ingredients}(f). Thus their scalar relation \(E\) is effective PA, has a common large-gain target pump and a large-loss source pump. The zeroizers are auxiliary proof gadgets and their internal heights do not redefine \(W\).

Split \(E\) into \(E_+,E_0,E_-\) with a sufficiently large scalar gain cutoff \(\Cs\). The bounded band \(E_0\) has finitely many differences, with unary PA enabling guards. Its closure together with the exact lower symmetric two-CM subsystem has an exact symmetric two-CM compiler. At scalar port \(p\) encode \(n\) as \((x,0)\), \(n=x+c_p\), where \(c_p\) is a fixed state-dependent offset, including at a canonical hub. Track the \emph{encoded} total \(x+y\) modulo a common guard modulus through every lower-subsystem transition; symmetry preserves the total update, so this finite-control refinement preserves symmetry. At the port it gives \(x\bmod M\) and hence \(n\bmod M\). Unary PA guards are finite exceptions and threshold/residue tails; fixed decrement/restore words and zero tests implement the finite threshold checks. The scalar shift \(m=n+d\) becomes \(x'=x+d+c_p-c_q\), with the unused counter tested zero to force its orientation. Consequently the combined lower closure is effectively semilinear by \textbf{S2}. No arbitrary PA binary relation is compiled into two counters.

For \(E_+\), the image fibers are \(D\)-upward, point membership and residue-domain profiles are PA, and a fixed lower gain bound holds. \(E_-\) has the reversed properties. Writing \(L_{\mathrm{low}}\) for the lower-subsystem relation, the scalar saturation theorem now decides

\begin{equation*}
\bigl(I;(E_0\cup E_+\cup E_-\cup L_{\mathrm{low}})^*\bigr)\cap J\neq\varnothing.
\end{equation*}


For completeness, that theorem uses exactly the same two saturations as Section~\ref{sec:completion}: first compute backward closure through negative transitions, then forward closure through positive transitions, using their upward fibers and effective PA image/preimage operations. PA operations for a positive relation follow from its lower gain bound and domain profiles: bounded targets allow finitely many source tests, while unbounded target residues meet every nonempty upward fiber. The middle lower relation is already reflexive-transitive and PA. No full binary or unary reachability relation for the whole scalar closure is assumed computed.

Together with the no-axis case this decides \(K(S,T)\) for every two boxes. Query-dependent finite pattern bounds change the enumeration, never the relation \(K\).

\section{Conclusion and scope}\label{sec:conclusion}

Section~\ref{sec:normal} supplies a finite exact event/kernel presentation of the original machine. Section~\ref{sec:oracle} supplies the box oracle for that \emph{same} \(K\) using only \textbf{S2}, \textbf{S3}, and effective finite/Presburger constructions. Section~\ref{sec:completion} therefore decides reachability between the original source and target singleton boxes.

Every saturation terminates by the ascending-chain condition for upward sets. Every graph/pattern bound is effective. The construction does not use a presumed algorithm for unrestricted three-counter reachability, a minimum-credit oracle, or semilinearity of the full three-counter relation.

This establishes the stated conditional decidability theorem. It does not establish effective semilinearity of symmetric three-counter reachability; the earlier exponential and quadratic examples in the research log rule out that stronger blanket conclusion.
