
We thank the reviewers for their effort and comments and suggestions - we'll 
take them all into account.

In summary, the major reviewers' objections concern:

1) insufficient motivation (#B,#C).

2) structure of the paper - insufficient explanation of main concepts 
   in the 10-page prefix (#C).

Our response:

1) We think that studying symmetry is of fundamental relevance. 
   It is in fact quite surprising that we do not know yet if the so natural 
   restriction on CM, namely counter symmetry, yields a Turing-complete model!

2) We agree, and propose an improvement.


Review #A:

A: We appreciate the final evaluation: 
   "this paper solves elegantly a well-motivated problem". 

Q: l 476: what is e? 
   l 481: The sentence starting with Wlog is unclear. I don't understand 
   what you mean by one-off.

A: e is the poly bound on lengths of LPSes [2]; 
   one-off = single-use (inside some path t_i, not on any cycle c_i).


Review #B:

Q: At high level, the techniques are not particularly novel, but 
   as always the details are the important thing, and the mathematical 
   work carried out here is nontrivial

A: Indeed, part of our technical contribution is to subtly adjust 
   mathematical tools (eg. Sect.4). On the other hand the concept of 
   target pumping (Lemma 9) is completely novel, and specific to 
   symmetric VASS.

Q: The motivation of the paper is not particularly strong [...] not 
   clear whether this model captures any natural system

A: In our opinion, impact of model symmetry on complexity of decision 
   problems seems a highly intriguing research question. We envisage 
   further research plan concerning partial symmetry, beyond just full 
   symmetry studied in this paper, that would presumably allow capturing 
   natural systems.


Review #C:

Q: I primarily find the question interesting because of the result that 
   shows a sharp contrast to the conventional case, however I am not 
   sure I fully see the motivation of the model beyond this. 

A: Even this single motivation is sufficient in our opinion: we deserve
   knowing whether imposing symmetry on CM yields a Turing-complete model 
   or not. 

Q: I do not feel the paper does a good job of providing the key arguments 
   in the first 10 pages. In particular, while the key results and 
   structure are presented in section 3, there is little intuition 
   of how section 5 and 6 will work before 10 pages. There are both 
   of the fundamental sections, and this is especially relevant for 
   Lemma 9 proven in section 6 which is fundamental to the argument.

A: We agree. Indeed, the key result is the proof of target pumping in 
   Sect. 6, whose underlying ideas are not sufficiently explained beforehand.
   We propose to move Lemma 9 (target pumping) to the end of Sect.3,
   and add a (say, 1-page) subsection that would sketch the most important 
   ideas behind the proof of Lemmas 6,7 and 9 and their inter-dependences.

Q: While a complexity upper bound is shown, it would be good to have 
   a matching lower bound.

A: Establishing tight complexity seems very challenging. We would 
   rather believe that the upper bound may be significantly improved, 
   maybe even to PSPACE.

Q: it is natural to ask for 3 counters, but the papers extension to 
   3-CM focuses on a different group structure to the main body.

A: Absolutely, the status of symmetric 3-CM, and generally symmetric n-CM, 
   is a very intriguing and natural question. We would love to answer 
   these question, and conjecture decidability in Sect.1. It seems however
   that even the case of 3-CM is extremely demanding. We have some
   partial decidability results, but still do not know the full answer. 
   We've looked at the cycle group to establish some frontiers for
   decidability, as symmetric group reduces to cyclic one.
